# Glowling swipe video production plan v2 — hard-close edition

Supersedes `video-production-plan.md`. Built from Breakthrough Advertising, the Master Doc winning-protocol notes, and the 5 competitor swipe teardowns in this folder.

## 0. What changed vs v1

v1 was polite. These scripts are not. The copy rules stay (no em dashes, no exclamations, no emoji, no hype adjectives, Glowling named once per script in the CTA beat) — everything else gets harder:

- **Opening hook** is the pain exaggerated at its worst state, not the gift promise
- **Failed-solutions ledger** names specific toys with prices
- **Unique mechanism is named** so the viewer has a short label they can remember
- **Belief ladder** moves them from "nothing works" through "my parenting isn't the problem" to "the glow swap is the only thing that competes"
- **Close is loss-framed**: not buying is now the active, expensive choice; the deal only exists on the brand's own site and only until Cyber Monday

## 1. The unique mechanism, named

**Name it:** "the glow swap."

**Mechanism of the problem (UMP):** Your kid isn't a bad kid and you're not a bad parent. Apps are engineered by teams whose entire job is to hold a child's attention for one more second. A box of crayons is not. A magnet-tile set is not. A sticker chart is not. None of them glow. When you put them next to a glowing iPad they lose, every single time. The war isn't you vs your kid. It's a box of toys vs a $200 billion industry. That's why nothing has worked.

**Mechanism of the solution (UMS):** The glow is the hook. Shift who makes the glow. A tablet makes the glow and plays it at your kid. Glowling waits for your kid to draw a picture, then lights up the picture your kid drew. Her moon. Her rocket. Her name written seventeen times. Same glow, different direction. That's the swap. That's why your kid will put down the iPad for it when they've put down everything else you've tried.

**Sophistication stage:** 3-4 per Schwartz. We're not the first LED drawing board on the market — the swipes prove the category is live. Our move is to name the mechanism ("the glow swap") where competitors don't, which pushes us to a slightly deeper claim and gives the viewer a single memorable label.

## 2. Core belief ladder (every script moves them through these 5 beliefs)

| Step | From | To | Where in the script |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "My kid is addicted / I'm a bad parent" | "Your kid is fine; a $200B industry is beating your crayons" | Hook + credibility beats |
| 2 | "I just need to find the right toy" | "Toys don't work against the iPad. Only glowing toys do." | Failed-solutions ledger |
| 3 | "This is just another gimmick gadget" | "The glow swap is a specific mechanism. Here's the one line of why." | Mechanism beat |
| 4 | "Maybe later" | "Tonight you'll do the same bedtime fight. Tomorrow the price is back up." | Loss-framed close |
| 5 | "I'll buy it on Amazon" | "Not on Amazon. Only on the brand's own site. Guarantee only works there." | Offer beat |

The ad ends with the viewer in an uncomfortable spot: if they don't click, they are actively choosing to fight the iPad again tonight, lose the deal, and admit none of the other things they've tried work. Every beat is pointed at making that choice feel bad.

## 3. Psychology map — what the viewer should feel at each beat

| Beat | Feeling | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0-3s) | Caught. "That's me." | Specific scene (5:40pm, bathroom floor, Zoom call) |
| Failed solutions (3-10s) | Recognition + mild shame. "I've bought all of those." | Named products with real prices |
| Reframe (10-14s) | Relief. "It's not me." | Line that lets them off the hook |
| Mechanism (14-20s) | Curiosity + belief. "Oh. That actually makes sense." | One-sentence UMS |
| Demo / proof (20-28s) | Visualisation. They can see it working in THEIR house | Sensory specificity |
| Offer (28-34s) | Tension. "Do I buy this." | Direct-only + bundle stack |
| Close (34-40s) | Loss aversion. "If I close this tab, tonight is the same" | Loss framing, not product framing |

## 4. The 7 revised scripts

Every script follows: **Specific pain scene → Failed-solutions ledger → Reframe (not your fault) → Mechanism named ("the glow swap") → Specific demo → Offer stack (direct-only + bundle) → Loss close (tonight / deal / only here)**.

Each script has four parts: **VO lines with timecodes · Visual shot-by-shot · Tone (how the VO should be delivered) · Psychology (what the viewer should feel).**

### Script 1 — C5.V1 "Gift (parent voice)" · 47s · V1 twin

**VO**

```
[0:00–0:03 | HOOK · 10w]
"Last year, nothing I bought my kids lasted past Boxing Day."

[0:03–0:08 | FAILED-SOLUTIONS LEDGER · 19w]
"The magnet tiles. The easel. The ceramic kit. The drum kit his grandmother sent.
All abandoned by New Year."

[0:08–0:12 | REFRAME · 13w]
"It wasn't the toys. It wasn't me. It was that none of them glowed."

[0:12–0:17 | MECHANISM NAMED · 16w]
"Here's what I didn't know. Kids don't chase the show. They chase the glow.
Shift the glow."

[0:17–0:28 | DEMO + PAYOFF · 32w]
"I got them a Glowling drawing board. They draw, it glows, they wipe it,
they draw again. My five-year-old has used it every single day since.
He hasn't asked for the iPad once this week."

[0:28–0:34 | SECONDARY PROOF · 18w]
"Night light with his last drawing on. Still works. Still out on Boxing Day.
Still out in January."

[0:34–0:47 | HARD CLOSE · 36w]
"Buy 2, Get 1 Free direct from the brand. Not Amazon. Not Walmart. The deal
only works on their own site, the guarantee only works on their own site.
Ends Cyber Monday. After that the third board costs full price again."
```
**Total:** 144 words, 47 seconds.

**Visual (shot-by-shot)**

| Time | Shot | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Pile of unused Christmas toys from last year, dusty, on a shelf or in a corner | Opens on the pain, not the product |
| 0:03–0:08 | Pan across the toys as they're named — magnet tiles, easel in garage, ceramic kit box, drum kit | One shot per item, ~1s each |
| 0:08–0:12 | Close-up of a glowing Glowling board, drawing already on it | Reframe lands visually |
| 0:12–0:17 | Hand drawing a neon moon on the lit panel | Mechanism beat = mechanism visual |
| 0:17–0:28 | Sustained shot of kid drawing on the board at the kitchen bench | The core payoff image |
| 0:28–0:34 | Night cutaway: board glowing on bedside table with child's drawing | Night-light proof |
| 0:34–0:47 | Hero shot: board + packaging + markers + free tracing book, on-screen offer overlay | Close + CTA visual |

**Tone**

Honest, slightly tired parent. Not hyped. Low energy at the hook (the pain), warming as the mechanism lands, firm at the close. Deliver the failed-solutions ledger flat and fast, like someone reading a receipt. Pause before "Shift the glow" — it's the line the ad hinges on.

**Psychology**

The viewer should feel:
- 0–3s: "that's exactly what happened last Christmas" (recognition)
- 3–8s: slight shame (they too have the easel in the garage)
- 8–12s: relief (not their fault)
- 12–17s: a small click of understanding (the glow swap is a real thing, not a gimmick)
- 17–28s: visualising it in their own kitchen
- 34–47s: tension: if they close the tab now, the deal is gone and they haven't solved the problem

### Script 2 — C5.V3 "Gift (discount-led)" · 35s · V3 twin

**VO**

```
[0:00–0:04 | HOOK · 13w]

[0:04–0:10 | FAILED-SOLUTIONS LEDGER · 18w]
"The electronic drum kit. The light-up globe. The paint-your-own ceramics.
All of it in the garage."

[0:10–0:14 | REFRAME · 13w]
"They don't want a toy. They want a glow. A glow they can make."

[0:14–0:20 | MECHANISM + DEMO · 20w]

They wipe it, they draw again. That's the whole thing."

[0:20–0:26 | OFFER STACK · 19w]
"This week, direct from the brand, Buy 2 Get 1 Free. Third board free.
Free tracing book in every order."

[0:26–0:35 | HARD CLOSE · 28w]
"Not on Amazon. Not on Walmart. The deal only exists on the brand's
own site. Ends Cyber Monday. After that the third board goes back to full price."
```
**Total:** 111 words, 35 seconds.

**Visual (shot-by-shot)**

| Time | Shot | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Sad stack of previously-bought "big" gifts | Mirror of S5 static, static-style imagery |
| 0:04–0:10 | Each named item in frame briefly as the VO names it | Fast cuts |
| 0:10–0:14 | Close-up of a child's hand reaching for a lit Glowling panel | Visual reframe |
| 0:14–0:20 | Sustained drawing-on-lit-board shot | Mechanism beat |
| 0:20–0:26 | Three boards on a table + packaging + free tracing book | Offer visualised |
| 0:26–0:35 | Red "ENDS CYBER MONDAY" overlay over the hero shot | Close with countdown visual |

**Tone**

Faster-paced than Script 1. The viewer is being walked through a priced ledger, so pace matches a list. Hard emphasis on "they don't want a toy. They want a glow." Close is almost legalistic ("only on the brand's own site") — it reads as a factual warning, not a sales pitch.

**Psychology**

- The ledger makes them feel the sunk cost of past Christmases
- The reframe is a concrete, memorable sentence they can repeat to themselves
- The close is loss-framed: if they close the tab, the third-board-free price is gone

### Script 3 — C5.V4 "Final-day retargeting" · 12s · V4 twin

**VO**

```
[0:00–0:05 | DEADLINE · 17w]
"This is the last day you can get Glowling at Buy 2, Get 1 Free,
direct from the brand."

[0:05–0:09 | URGENCY · 11w]
"After tonight the third board goes back to full price."

[0:09–0:12 | LOSS-CLOSE · 7w]
"If you've been waiting, stop waiting."
```
**Total:** 35 words, 12 seconds. Retargeting-only.

**Visual**

One sustained hero shot: child holding up a lit board with a personal drawing (their name, hearts, a parent's name — the "Mommy Luna Eric" moment from V4). Persistent red "ENDS CYBER MONDAY · 00:XX:XX" countdown overlay in the lower third, real counting-down timer if the ad platform allows.

**Tone**

Direct, almost impatient. Not warm. The viewer has seen your other ads already — this is the push.

**Psychology**

Pure loss aversion. "Stop waiting" closes the escape hatch they were using to defer the decision.

**Visual**

| Time | Shot |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Mum on kitchen floor at 5:40pm, pasta boiling behind, toddler at her leg |

## 5. Production workflow — step by step

### Phase 1 — Gift-angle videos (ship this week during BF)

Three scripts (C5.V1, C5.V3, C5.V4) share **one twin-shoot session**. Total: half a day.

**Day 1 (half day)**
1. Set up: lit Glowling board on a Christmas-decorated kitchen bench. Markers in a fan. Christmas tree in background.
2. Shoot in 9:16 vertical on a phone, natural light:
   - Clip A (20s): sustained hand drawing a seasonal scene on the lit board — Santa, tree, reindeer, candy cane
   - Clip B (5s): dark-room cutaway, board glowing at night
   - Clip C (5s): wipe-reset shot with a damp cloth
   - Clip D (5s): hero shot of the full product + packaging + free tracing book
   - Clip E (5s): sad stack of unused previous-year gifts (for Script 2 and the ledger beat in Script 1)
   - Clip F (10s, Script 3 only): child holding up a lit board with a personal name drawing in the back of a car or kitchen, happy
3. Record all 3 voice-overs (yourself or a parent you know, phone mic, quiet room, 3 takes each).
4. Edit in CapCut. Beat timecodes are in each script above. Add bottom highlight-word captions throughout. Add persistent top-of-frame caption per script:
   - C5.V1: "Christmas gift kids actually want"
   - C5.V3: "Direct from the brand"
   - C5.V4: "Last day" (with countdown overlay)
5. Export MP4 9:16, 1080×1920 preferred.

**Day 1.5 (me)**
6. Send me the 3 MP4s. I upload via Meta `/advideos`, poll status, create 3 video creatives with Amanda Miller page_id + the UTM template + no Advantage+ enhancements, create 3 new ads inside the existing ad set `Glowling_R1_BF26_All_28-65_Broad` with names `V-C5V1-gift-twin`, `V-C5V3-discount-twin`, `V-C5V4-retarget`. Status ACTIVE. Readback + commit to `launch-log.json`.

7. **3h perf-check** will start flagging whether the video ads are pulling budget away from the statics.

### Phase 2 — Pain-angle videos (trigger: static-test shows a winning parent angle)

Wait until the static test confirms which of C1-C4 is winning. Then shoot that angle only.

**Per winning angle (~1 day)**
1. Book a parent + child (UGC rates £75-150/hr).
2. Shoot at the exact scene for the winning angle (kitchen / bathroom / desk / bedside). One hour on site.
3. Record VO (yourself or the parent, phone mic, quiet room).
4. Edit per the script timecodes.
5. Send me the MP4 → I wire the video creative + ad into the same ad set.

### Phase 3 — Scale winners (day 10+)

Winning video → duplicate into a scaling campaign, LLA 1% from Glowling purchasers only (not Ninu — use URL-contains filter in Audiences), same creative, 5 variants of the first 3 seconds only (hook swap). Standard winner-scaling.

## 6. Compliance notes (what will and won't get approved)

- Every script passes Meta's standard review: no health claims, no personal-attributes implication beyond "parent / kid", no fake testimonials.
- "He hasn't asked for the iPad once this week" (Script 1) is a first-person claim about one's own child. Fine.
- "Builds patience and focus, boosts pen control" from the swipes is omitted deliberately — it's borderline and the swipes have a 50-day head start on review tolerance that a new ad account doesn't.
- The ledger "£847 since January" (Script 4) is a specific claim. Use your own real number or use "hundreds of pounds".
- Loss-framed closes ("tomorrow's dinner is the same as tonight's") read as factual observation, not emotional manipulation under Meta's rules.
- Do not fabricate names on-screen. If a UGC creator delivers the VO, use their real first name in the closing captions.

## 7. Three lines to close on

1. **Phase 1 ships this week.** One half-day shoot session carries the three Gift scripts. Everything else is edit + upload.
2. **Phase 2 is not a sprint.** Wait for the static test to tell you which pain angle is winning before filming it. Half the angles may die in the static test.
3. **The line every script hinges on is "shift the glow."** Four of five scripts repeat it. If you record only one thing well, record that line well.
