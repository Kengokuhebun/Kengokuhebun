> No product intake file, competitor list, or customer data was supplied. Every field below runs on category-standard assumptions for a portable ozone-gas shoe deodorizer (comparable to devices like SteriShoe or generic Amazon ozone units), flagged as such rather than presented as sourced data.

## Step 1 — Product intake (assumed)

- **Category:** personal-care device
- **Mechanism:** rechargeable device sits inside an empty shoe/boot/bag and emits ozone (O₃), oxidizing odor-causing bacteria and fungal spores at the source rather than masking smell
- **Claims:** kills 99%+ of odor-causing bacteria, no chemicals/fragrance residue, reduces fungal-growth conditions, safe on leather/mesh/rubber/insulated materials
- **Price point:** $25–50, single or 2-pack
- **Assumed current buyer:** gym-goers, recreational athletes, gift buyers for teen sons
- **How competitors market it:** functional Amazon-listing copy ("kills bacteria," "eliminates odor"), gym/locker-room creative, novelty-gift framing — no profession- or hobby-specific messaging in the category
- **Ad platforms:** Meta, TikTok
- **Market:** United States

## Step 2 — Market mapping

**2A Core problem:** shoes trap moisture/bacteria until they smell; underneath is fear of being judged or embarrassed; cost is social avoidance, damaged shoes from over-washing, real fungal/health risk in closed-toe jobs.

**2B Where it occurs:** anywhere shoes come off in front of others (gyms, mats, sleepovers, dating) and anywhere shoes stay closed 8–12+ hours (hospitals, kitchens, construction, rideshares) — plus contexts where odor has a second consequence beyond embarrassment (hunting, fungal infection, closed vehicle cabins).

**2C Existing solutions and why they fail:** sprays/powders mask rather than kill; washing ruins cleats/leather/insulated boots; baking soda/cedar/freezing are inconsistent folk remedies. The frustration gap: nothing on the market treats the bacteria, only the smell.

**2D Who competitors ignore:** anyone whose odor problem is tied to a profession, hobby, or someone else's kid rather than "athlete" — shift workers in code-mandated footwear, hunters (for whom odor is a hunt-ending liability, not embarrassment), and parents, who are the actual purchaser but never the one addressed in the copy.

## Step 5 — Top 3 deep dives

### 1. Scent-Locked Steve — Bowhunter (20/25, Goldmine)

- **Profile:** Works full-time, hunts a handful of high-stakes weekends a year, has already invested heavily in scent-free soap, sprayed outerwear, and scent-free storage totes — but boots are the weak link, worn for hours and thrown in the mudroom afterward.
- **Mass desire:** To become undetectable to prey — mastery over the one variable skill alone can't control, and the pride of a clean, earned harvest.
- **Voice of customer:** "I've done everything right and still get busted by wind and scent." / "Spent two grand on this hunt — not letting my boots blow it." / "I run an Ozonics in the truck, but my boots sit in the mudroom stinking up everything." / "If it doesn't actually kill the bacteria, it's not scent control, it's cologne."
- **Hooks:** Pain — "You did everything right. Then your boots gave you away." Identity — "Serious hunters don't mask scent. They eliminate it." Curiosity/mechanism — "The same ozone tech in $300 scent-control machines now fits in your boot."
- **Offer positioning:** "Undetectable from the ground up." Portable/rechargeable, true elimination not masking, safe on rubber/leather/insulated boots, overnight cycle.
- **Objection:** "I already run an Ozonics." → Ozonics treats real-time air in the stand; this treats bacteria embedded in boot material between hunts — preventive, not redundant.
- **Meta targeting:** Realtree, Mossy Oak, Cabela's, Bass Pro Shops, Ozonics, Scent Killer, MeatEater, Cameron Hanes, Whitetail Properties.

### 2. The 12-Hour Shift Nurse (19/25, Goldmine)

- **Profile:** Twelve hours on concrete in dress-code-mandated closed-toe shoes (Dansko/Hoka/Skechers Work); clinically literate about bacteria, so skeptical of vague claims but persuadable by a real mechanism; shoes get banished to the garage.
- **Mass desire:** To feel clean and in control at the end of a shift that already asks everything of her body.
- **Voice of customer:** "I can smell my own shoes by hour eight." / "My husband makes me leave my shoes in the garage." / "I wash my hands a hundred times a shift — why not my shoes?" / "I need something I trust actually kills germs, not just covers it up."
- **Hooks:** Pain — "12-hour shift. Zero break for your feet. Your shoes shouldn't suffer for it either." Identity — "You kill germs for a living. Your shoes deserve the same standard." Curiosity/mechanism — "The disinfecting tech hospitals trust — now sized for your work shoes."
- **Offer positioning:** "Built for the 12-hour shift." Plug-in-and-forget overnight cycle, fits Dansko/Hoka/Skechers Work, fragrance-free.
- **Objection:** "Is ozone safe to breathe, will it damage my shoes?" → Runs in a sealed, unworn shoe and dissipates before the shoe is worn again — same category as hospital terminal-room disinfection tech.
- **Meta targeting:** Scrubs & Beyond, FIGS, Dansko, Hoka, Nurse.org, travel nursing agencies, "Nurse Life" pages.

### 3. The Protective Sports Parent (17/25, Priority)

- **Profile:** Drives carpool with the windows cracked, has warned other parents before sleepovers, has ruined shoes trying to machine-wash the smell out; wants a fix that doesn't depend on her kid remembering anything.
- **Mass desire:** To shield her kid from social embarrassment before it happens.
- **Voice of customer:** "My son's cleats can clear a room." / "He got teased once about his shoes and now he won't take them off at school." / "I just want to plug something in at night and not think about it."
- **Hooks:** Pain — "One joke about smelly shoes can wreck a kid's confidence for a whole school year." Identity — "You handle everything else for your kid. Let this handle the shoe smell." Curiosity/mechanism — "It's not a spray and it's not baking soda — it's odor-killing tech, shrunk down for sneakers."
- **Offer positioning:** "The 5-minute bedtime habit for shoes (and confidence) that stay fresh." Chemical-free, works on cleats/sneakers/dance shoes, doesn't depend on the kid.
- **Objection:** "Is this safe for my kid?" → Runs on an empty, unworn shoe with no skin contact and dissipates before it's worn again — the opposite of a spray that touches the shoe interior directly.
- **Meta targeting:** Youth soccer/football/cheer pages, "Moms of Boys/Girls" groups, Scary Mommy, PTA groups, team parent pages.

**Recommendation:** Launch with the hunter avatar — highest TAM score, highest purchasing power, zero direct competition on this exact positioning, and a mechanism story ("eliminate, don't mask") that matches how serious hunters already think about scent control. Once CPA/ROAS benchmarks are proven, move to the nurse/healthcare avatar — a near-identical TAM score, and a segment whose clinical trust in "kills bacteria" claims should convert efficiently off lookalikes from the first campaign. Run the sports-parent avatar as a third, separate campaign — it converts on a protective emotional angle, not a mechanism-first hook, so it needs its own creative rather than a shared ad set. Keep all three as distinct campaigns so Meta's delivery algorithm can optimize per audience, then scale whichever shows the strongest early CTR/CVR.
