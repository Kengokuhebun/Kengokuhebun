## The promise to sell (from the evidence)
Don't promise "replaces the iPad." The credible promise comes from a parent's own words in r/Parenting:
> "It doesn't replace TV completely… But it does buy me a little quiet time before the screen comes out, and honestly that's good enough."

**Positioning:** *the thing you try first, before the screen.* It's honest, it fits the native-ad pattern (absolution, not perfection), and it can't be disproved by a single bad afternoon.

## Step 1: Product test (before spending on ads), 7–10 days
Order 2–3 supplier samples (ideally 2 different suppliers) and test with 3–5 real kids aged 3–5. Record:
1. **Independent minutes** on day 1, day 3 and day 7 (timer starts when the adult walks away; stops at the first call for help, or when the child leaves).
2. **Surface wear:** 20 draw-and-wipe cycles with the recommended cloth. Photograph scratches and ghosting with the light on.
3. **Marker life:** how many full drawings before the first marker fades.
4. **Light:** any flicker or failure; charger compatibility.
5. **Mess:** marker on hands, clothes or table; how easily it washes.

**Go / no-go:** median independent time on day 7 **≥ 10 minutes** and no visible scratching after 20 wipes. If it fails, change supplier or pen type (liquid chalk vs fluorescent marker) before launching.
Bonus: with the parents' permission, film the sessions. That's your first real UGC and the source of real reviews for the product page.

## Copy rules carried into ads and the product page
- Never claim it replaces screens, calms meltdowns, helps ADHD or autism, or improves development.
- OK to say: it lights up, no app, no ads, no autoplay, they make something, wipe and reuse, night light, "something to try first".
- Use the market's own words: "no village", "independent play", "need quiet for a call", "cries the entire time I'm in the shower", "5 mins".
- Age: lead with **3–5** for Avatar A. Under-3s plus markers is a mess and scratch risk.
