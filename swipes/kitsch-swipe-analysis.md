## 2. The winning pattern (common to both)

1. **The applicator is the hero, not the oil.** The oil is a commodity. The tool is the new thing.
2. **The desire is delivery, not ingredients.** Precision, even coverage, direct to the roots, less mess.
3. **It upgrades a ritual the viewer already believes in.** Neither video sells "start oiling." Both sell "oil better."
4. **The mess/imprecision of the old way is named in visceral words:** "freaking messy," "uneven," "dousing."
5. **Ease is the emotional payoff.** "Easy and effective," "I can do this whenever."
6. **Formats are plain UGC demos.** Hands in hair, burned-in captions, bathroom light, no studio polish.

This is the same desire as our **Angle 1 (Upgrade the Ritual You Already Trust)**. It's the one angle in our set that these two winners directly validate.

## 3. What transfers, and what doesn't

| Transfers | Does not transfer |
|---|---|
| Applicator as hero; "oil better" not "oil more" | "Metal roller balls," "cooling massage," "mini spa treatment." Our tips are hollow silicone and dispense through the tip, so this isn't our mechanism |
| Naming the dropper/finger mess in blunt words | "Stimulate the hair growth," "grow long, strong, healthy." Our guardrail is no regrowth or medical claims |
| "Start here and go around" demo logic | "Sold out, then restocked, not many left." We have no sales history. Use scarcity only if it's true |
| Ease and permission as the payoff | Kitsch's avatar is broad beauty-routine. Ours is postpartum and must carry that emotion |
| Works with any oil (our **Dispenser Only** tier is the match) | "Pumpkin seed oil capsules" trick, unless we've tested it on our applicator |
| Price as a reason ("really affordable") | Their "NEW LAUNCH!" badge (only if the launch is real) |

**Our own differentiators to lean on:**
- The clear chamber has 1-4ml markings, so she can see how much she's using. That's a direct answer to "dousing," and Kitsch doesn't show it.
- Our avatar's context makes the mess worse: one hand, 20 minutes, a baby monitor on the counter, a towel to wash.

## 5. Iterated hooks, leads and CTAs (draft)

All three call out "moms," not "your thinning hair," to stay inside Meta's personal-attributes rules. None makes a regrowth or medical claim.

### Ad A: temple/hairline panic (ugly shock native)

### Ad C: wasting the oil (Facebook share / news headline). Closest to the proven swipe.
- **Hook (headline):** "Because of one small tool, moms are finally keeping up their scalp oil routine."
- **Lead:** "The dropper was messy. Fingers were uneven. Half the oil ended up on the towel. Here's what's changing that."

*(Hook C adapts Vid 2's "because of this little gadget, I actually use my ___" frame to our avatar.)*

## 6. Compliance and honesty notes

- The first-person leads above read as a real mom's story. If they run as a "real mom" testimonial, the story has to be real. Otherwise write them as the brand's voice ("Moms tell us...") only if true, or use real UGC.
- Don't use "sold out" or "restocked" unless it's true.
- Mechanism claims should match the product we actually ship: hollow silicone tips, clear chamber with ml markings.

### What Ads 1 and 2 add to the pattern

**1. The winning hook is a fear of thinning, not only ease.** "Your Bald Spot's Worst Nightmare" names the pain directly, and the proof pill ("It sold out 5 times") supports it. The three benefit bullets then sell the same delivery desire as the UGC videos:
- Precise, mess-free application, no oil dripping down your forehead
- Quick and effortless routine
- Targets your scalp, not your hair, for less waste and better application

So the proven formula is a fear-of-thinning hook plus three delivery/ease bullets. That supports both our **Ad A (temple panic) as the hook** and the **delivery mechanism as the shared body**.

**2. Primary text is short, with an emoji bullet list.** Ad 2's copy is one hook line and four short benefit lines. It is not longform.

**3. Kitsch leans on trust proofs we don't have.** "Sold out 5 times," "award-winning," "everyone is raving," and a 90-day return policy are all trust signals. This is the same trust gap behind Velmir's ATC → purchase drop. We can swipe the desire, not their credibility.

### What not to copy

- **"Your bald spot."** It tells the viewer something about her body, which Meta's personal-attributes policy can reject, even though Kitsch is running it. Use the same "X's worst nightmare" frame on the problem: "Your messy oil routine's worst nightmare."
- **"Sold out 5 times," "award-winning," "everyone is raving."** We have no basis for these.
- **"Improve scalp health," "reduce frizz & breakage."** These are claims we don't make.
- **Their bullets word for word.** Rewrite them for our product.
