# Avatar Deepening  

Inputs

PRODUCT: \[what it is, and its mechanism in one sentence\]

PROBLEM IT SOLVES: \[the specific problem, with its clinical or industry name if it has one\]

CURRENT AVATAR DRAFT: \[who you think you're selling to — one or two sentences\]

BUYER OR SUFFERER: \[is the buyer the person with the problem, or someone buying on their behalf?\]

DEMOGRAPHIC ASSUMPTIONS: \[age range, gender, life stage — Claude will check these against reality\]

CONTEXT / SETTING: \[the activity, industry, role, or life situation the problem happens inside\]

WINNING DESIRE (if known): \["I want \_\_\_" statement\]

KNOWN COMMUNITIES: \[subreddits or forums you already know of, if any\]

  

\# INSTRUCTIONS

  

You are running the final deepening pass on an avatar the user has already researched. Your job is not to discover who the buyer is. It is to make the buyer \*specific\* enough that the ads hit deeper than the competition's.

  

\#\# HARD RULES

1\. \*\*Browse. Do not answer from knowledge.\*\* Every finding must come from a page you actually opened and read this session. If you catch yourself writing something you did not read, delete it.

2\. \*\*Verbatim only.\*\* Quotes must be copied word for word, typos preserved. Never paraphrase into quotation marks. Never invent a quote to illustrate a point. One fabricated quote poisons every ad written downstream.

3\. \*\*Link everything.\*\* Every thread you read gets a clickable URL in the sources list, with its comment count.

4\. \*\*Read full threads, not search snippets.\*\* Search results tell you what exists. The findings live in the comments.

5\. \*\*Flag thin evidence explicitly.\*\* If a dimension has three quotes instead of thirty, say so in the output. "I found only two examples of this" is worth more than a confident paragraph built on nothing.

6\. \*\*Verify no statistics.\*\* If you cannot trace a number to a primary source, leave it out entirely rather than hedging it.

7\. \*\*Correct the user's assumptions out loud.\*\* If the research contradicts the age range, the demographic, the setting, or the avatar draft they gave you, say so plainly and show the evidence. This is one of the most valuable things this pass produces.

  

\#\# RESEARCH PROTOCOL

\*\*Find the home community first.\*\* Search Reddit for the problem and identify where the people who have it actually congregate. There is usually one dominant subreddit plus two or three adjacent ones. Check whether the population posting is the person with the problem or someone acting on their behalf — those are different avatars with different vocabularies, and the user's input tells you which one you need.

  

\*\*Sort by comment count, not upvotes.\*\* The highest-signal threads for this work are often low-vote, high-comment. A post with 16 votes and 94 comments is a community working through something painful together. A post with 500 votes and 12 comments is a joke or a photo.

  

\*\*Run at least these six search angles\*\*, rewritten in the vocabulary of the niche:

\- The problem plus the relationship word the buyer would use — "my," "our," their role, their dependent

\- "what causes \[problem\]" or the two competing explanations the field argues about

\- "desperate" / "devastated" / "nothing is working" / "at my wits end" plus the problem — finds the emotional peak posts

\- "\[problem\] what actually helped" / "what worked for you" — finds the failed-solutions stack and any real relief

\- The escalation words for this niche — surgery, medication, legal, firing, quitting, second opinion — finds what they fear coming next

\- The problem plus a cost word: "expensive," "can't afford," "worth it" — finds economic constraints and resentment

  

\*\*Read a minimum of 4 threads in full\*\*, prioritising the largest comment counts. Read more if any dimension below is still thin.

  

\*\*Two specific things to hunt for, because they are where the money is:\*\*

\- \*\*The dissenting comment inside a causation thread.\*\* When twenty people give the same standard explanation and one person says "everyone's saying X, but almost everyone has X and most of them are fine" — that dissenter is pointing directly at the gap in the market's understanding. Find them.

\- \*\*Posts where the person has already RULED OUT the known causes.\*\* "I did everything right and it happened anyway." "I've tried all of this and none of it applies to me." These are the highest-value posts in the entire exercise, because they reveal whether you are fighting a wrong belief or filling a vacuum — and those two situations need completely different ads.

  

\#\# THE SEVEN QUESTIONS YOU MUST ANSWER

Answer each with sourced verbatim. If a question cannot be answered from what you read, say so rather than filling it in.

  

\*\*1. WHO, specifically.\*\* Age range (check the user's assumption against the ages that actually appear in posts), gender of the buyer and of the person with the problem, and the sub-segments inside the niche. Name any segment the user missed.

  

\*\*2. THE CONTEXT, in detail.\*\* Not the category — the specifics. Which level, which setting, which season or stage, which role, which competing pressures, which budget bracket. Generic context is why most ads feel like they're addressed to nobody. The specifics are what make a stranger feel seen.

  

\*\*3. FAILED SOLUTIONS — the complete stack.\*\* Everything they have already tried, roughly in the order they tried it. Include the free things and the expensive things, the DIY and the professional. This list is what your copy has to out-position, and it is usually longer than the user expects.

  

\*\*4. WHAT THEY ARE EMOTIONALLY TIRED OF — and be specific.\*\* Not "tired of the problem." Tired of \*what\*? Being dismissed by professionals? Advice from people who don't understand their situation? Things that work for everyone else but not for them? Feeling like a failure? Being told to spend money they don't have? Every answer being subtraction — do less, stop, wait, cut it out? Name the exact fatigue and quote it. This is usually the single most useful thing this pass produces.

  

\*\*5. WHAT THEY BELIEVE CAUSES IT.\*\* The community's dominant explanations, ranked by how often they appear. Quote them as they are actually given.

  

\*\*6. ⭐ WHAT THEY BELIEVE IS \*NOT\* THE CAUSE — THIS IS THE OPPORTUNITY.\*\* The belief they are missing is your opening. Look for the mechanism nobody in the community ever mentions. Then determine which of two situations you are in:

   - \*\*Wrong belief:\*\* they confidently hold an explanation that is incomplete or mistaken. Your ad has to break it, and the copy earns the right to correct them.

   - \*\*Vacuum:\*\* they have already personally ruled out the known causes and have no explanation left. They say things like "one of those things," "everyone's different," "I guess I'm just unlucky." Your ad has to \*fill\* it, and it should open by validating that they already did everything right.

   These require opposite ads. Opening with a correction when the reader is in a vacuum reads as an insult. State clearly which one you found, and the evidence for it.

  

\*\*7. WHAT THEY ARE AFRAID TO TRY NEXT.\*\* The escalation they can feel coming and don't want — a procedure, a medication, a diagnosis they don't want confirmed, a cost they can't absorb, giving the thing up entirely. This tells you what "not that" positioning is available to you, which is often the cleanest differentiation in the category.

  

\#\# OUTPUT FORMAT

Deliver in exactly this order.

  

\*\*1. THE PARAGRAPH.\*\* One dense, continuous paragraph, roughly 250–400 words, covering all seven questions with the strongest verbatim woven through it. Written so it can be handed to a copywriter or a video editor and used as-is. No bullet points, no headers inside it. This is the deliverable.

  

\*\*2. CORRECTIONS.\*\* Anything the research contradicted in the user's inputs — age, demographic, setting, segment, avatar draft. Show the evidence. Say plainly what to change.

  

\*\*3. THE STRATEGIC FINDING.\*\* One short section naming whether you found a wrong belief or a vacuum, and what that means for how the ad should open.

  

\*\*4. SOURCES.\*\* Every thread you read, as clickable markdown links, with comment counts. Mark which you read in full versus surfaced but did not open.

  

\*\*5. HONESTY NOTES.\*\* Which of the seven dimensions had thin support. Which searches came back empty. Any assumption you could not verify. Do not skip this section — it tells the user where not to bet.
