## THE PARAGRAPH (hand to the copywriter or editor)

She is a woman in her late 50s to 70s, often a few years past menopause. Her hair is thinner at the part and finer than it used to be, and she's buying for herself. Every night, or twice a day, she applies something to her scalp: rosemary or castor oil, or a minoxidil liquid or foam a doctor or friend suggested. She believed in the routine. The routine is what beats her. The product goes on her hair, not her scalp: "It still get caught in my hair and not my scalp lol" [U], and the instructions say not to let it touch the hair, "well that is impossible if you aren't bald" (woman, 75+) [V]. Her fine hair shows every drop: "it makes your hair dirty and I would only have to wash my hair once ecvery three days if I didn't use it" (woman, 55–64) [V]. Another writes, "whole time I used it, my hair was sticky" (woman, 65–74) [V]. So she washes more, or rations it: "can only use rosemary oil on the days I wash because it leaves my hair too greasy" [U]. It takes time and her body feels it: "my arms get tired from spending 20+ minutes on the process" [U]. She has tried the workarounds. One 75+ woman dabs it on "with a small cotton ball X2 per day" [V]. Others sets "an alarm set on my phone so I won't forget to apply it" (woman, 65–74) [V]. Others use a toothbrush, a Q-tip or a makeup brush in a cap, and then "it became such a chore [...] I stopped doing it as regularly" [U]. Even purpose-made combs disappoint: "got so messy and to clean it was a nightmare" [U]. What she's tired of isn't the hair loss. It's a routine that leaves her greasy, costs her time and makes her feel she's failing at something simple. So she cuts back to once a day, skips nights, or says "Might just quit" [U]. She hasn't been told the method is the problem. She assumes the mess comes with the product, or with her.

## Corrections to our inputs
1. **Age 55+:** supported by our buyers and WebMD. Reddit can't confirm it, because ages are rarely stated. Keep 55+, and keep the 65+ group in targeting.
2. **Hands and arthritis:** not a segment. Two quotes in total ([V] Kitsch, [U] bigger dropper). Use it as one line of copy at most ("one hand, no squeezing"), and only after checking how much pressure Ninu's top needs.
3. **The itchy-scalp "dismissed by doctors" theme** is real (`00b`), but it's a separate angle (M2). Don't mix it into the messy-routine story.

## Strategic finding: closer to a vacuum, with a self-blame twist
She doesn't hold a wrong theory about *why* application is messy. She has no theory. She treats the mess as unavoidable ("impossible if you aren't bald") and blames her own consistency ("I stopped doing it as regularly", "Might just quit").

**So the ad should open by validating her** ("I believed in it. I just couldn't keep doing it."). Then reveal the cause she never considered: **the delivery method puts the product on the hair, not the scalp.** That's exactly the shape of Ad C, the ad that converted: "I was never the problem. The method was."
