## Why most postpartum scalp routines fail by week two

If you're reading this, you've already done the hard part. You've admitted something is going on with your hair. You bought the castor oil, maybe the rosemary, probably some supplements. You started.

Then one night the baby cried, you came back ten minutes later, and you just lay down. You skipped. Then you skipped again. The bottle went to the back of the shelf. By the end of the month, the routine was gone and the shedding wasn't.

Here's what nobody told you: **the routine wasn't failing because of your discipline. It was failing because of the delivery method.**

A standard dropper, your fingers, a brush — they all route the oil through your hair *first*, before it has any chance of touching skin. And hair doesn't absorb oil. Only skin does. So every night you were spending five or ten minutes "doing the routine," almost none of the product was actually reaching the thing that could use it.

The shame, the stained pillowcases, the "I just can't do consistent things anymore" — none of that was you failing. It was the tool.

## What actually matters for scalp oil to work

Four things, in order of how much they matter.

**1. The oil has to touch skin, not hair.** This is the one everybody misses. Oil deposited on hair strands slides down, soaks into fabric, and never contacts the follicle. Direct skin contact is non-negotiable.

**2. Enough time in contact.** Most scalp oils need to sit on skin for several hours to be absorbed properly. Which is why the "massage it in and go to bed" rhythm works — if the oil is actually on skin to begin with.

**3. Consistency over intensity.** Three minutes every night beats forty minutes twice a week. Postpartum hormones are already doing their thing on a daily cycle — your routine needs to meet that cadence.

**4. The product itself.** Mattering last, not first. The right oil on the wrong delivery system still fails. The wrong oil on the right delivery system at least reaches your scalp.

Most content you'll see online inverts this list. They lead with the oil, then the massage technique, then maybe mention consistency, and never mention the delivery problem at all. That's why the advice doesn't work.

## Your nightly routine (two minutes, no mess)

This is the whole thing. If you've been doing something more complicated, you can stop.

### Step 1 — Part your hair

One straight part down the middle. Not for the result, just so the dispenser tips can reach the scalp cleanly without wading through hair first.

### Step 2 — Press

Hold the Ninu dispenser upright. Press the hollow silicone tips flat against the scalp along the part line. You'll feel them meet skin.

### Step 3 — Glide

Slow, gentle glide along the part — one pass is enough. The oil releases from the tips directly onto skin. You're not pumping, you're just letting gravity and the tip contact do the work. Don't press hard.

### Step 4 — Make a new part

Move the hair over an inch. Repeat the glide. Do this three or four times across the top of your head — temple to temple is more than enough.

### Step 5 — Hands off

Don't massage it in with your fingers afterward. That just routes the oil onto hair again and defeats the whole point. The oil is already on skin. Leave it alone.

### Step 6 — Bonnet, then bed

Satin bonnet on. This keeps the oil from migrating to your pillow and keeps your hair from frizzing against cotton while you sleep.

Total time: around two minutes.

## Things people worry about

**"Won't my hair be greasy?"**
Because the oil goes on skin, not strands, no — much less greasy than any other scalp oil method. If your hair ever looks oily, you're probably using too much. One or two tips' worth per section is plenty.

**"How often should I refill the oil?"**
A 60ml bottle lasts around 30 days of nightly use at 1-2ml a night. If you're using more than that, pull back.

**"Can I use my own oil?"**
Yes. The Ninu dispenser works with any oil thin enough to flow through the tips — castor is at the thicker end but still works; rosemary, argan, jojoba all work beautifully. The dispenser is the mechanism; the oil is a preference.

**"Do I need to wash my hair the next morning?"**
Only if you'd normally wash it that day. The oil is on scalp overnight; by morning most of it has absorbed. A quick shampoo refresh if you feel oily, but most mornings you won't.

**"What if I miss a night?"**
Then you miss a night. That's it. No reset. You pick it up the next evening. The whole point of this routine is it has to be easier than the reason to quit — which includes forgiving yourself when you quit for one night.
