## 4. The winning angle, desire and mechanism

- **Angle (the proven one, "Ad C"):** habit and delivery. **"The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit."**
- **Desire:** **"I was never the problem. The method was."** Also "I just wanted to feel like myself again."
- **Mechanism (the only explained one):** hair does not absorb oil, skin does; a dropper or fingers put most of it on the hair, neck and towel; hollow silicone tips press against the scalp along the part and release the oil directly on the skin. "One hand. Two minutes. Works with the oil I already had."
- **Compliance lane that has held since round 1:** behaviour and delivery claims only ("I hadn't skipped a night", "the oil releases right there"). **No hair regrowth, shedding-stopped, medical or study claims, ever.** First-person stories run as brand voice, not as verified testimonials.

### The winning body copy (Ad C, 430 words, from `ads/ninu-r8-hook-only.md`; it has converted in every execution)

> I actually do my scalp oil every night now. That wasn't true six months ago.
> I had the castor oil. I had the rosemary. I believed in the routine. I just couldn't make myself do it consistently.
> I'd do it four nights in a row and then the baby would be up three times and I'd get into bed with wet hands and just not do it. Then I'd skip a second night. The bottle would migrate to the back of the shelf and I'd tell myself I'd start over Sunday. I never started over Sunday.
> The thing I kept reading about, the consistent nightly scalp massage, the women who said they did it every single night for six months, I couldn't figure out how they were doing it. My routine was annoying. Oil on my fingers before I touched anything else. Running down my neck. Soaking into the towel I'd wrapped around my shoulders to protect my shirt. By the time I finished one section I was already thinking about the cleanup. It stopped feeling like self-care and started feeling like a chore.
> A chore at 10pm after a full day with a baby is a chore that gets skipped.
> Turns out I'd been putting it in the wrong place. Hair doesn't absorb oil. Skin does. Nights of working it down the strands, and almost none of it reached my scalp.
> I got a dispenser with hollow silicone tips. You press them against the scalp along the part and the oil releases right there, on the skin. Nothing runs. Nothing drips. Clear chamber, 1 to 4ml markings. One hand. Two minutes. Works with the oil I already had.
> The first night I used it I thought: oh. This is what it's supposed to feel like.
> By the end of the first week I hadn't skipped a night. That had never happened. A few weeks in the routine was automatic, same as washing my face. Not because I'd become more disciplined. Because the tool I'd been using was wrong and this one isn't.
> The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit.
> The Ninu Kit comes with the dispenser, 60ml scalp oil, and a satin bonnet for $49.99. Free US shipping and a 30-day money-back guarantee. [...] Make tonight the easy one. Try the Ninu Kit risk-free for 30 days.

## 8. Rules from the master document (summary)

- **Hooks:** three doors (pain, identity, curiosity or mechanism); the lead does not change when the hook does; one variable per round.
- **Native images:** look like something a person posted, not marketing; no fabricated people, results or reviews; a generated model is illustrative, not a customer endorsement.
- **Copy:** short paragraphs, first-person scene leads, no hedged identity qualifiers, no scaffolding phrases, no em dashes in new copy, every factual claim traceable to research or brand truth.
- **Urgency:** only a real deadline. No fake scarcity.
- **Avatar:** do not open with a correction when the reader is in a vacuum; validate first.

## 9. Compliance guardrails for organic content

1. Behaviour and delivery only. No hair regrowth, shedding, medical or "postpartum treatment" claims.
2. First-person stories are brand voice. If a real customer, tester or creator posts, they must have really used it, and paid or gifted partnerships must be disclosed.
3. No invented customers, comments, reviews, numbers or screenshots.
4. The price lines and the Nov 10 deadline must be true.
5. Don't use another brand's IP, trademarked sounds or competitors' footage.
6. Check platform rules (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Pinterest, YouTube Shorts) at the time of posting; the main session did not verify current policy text.

## 10. What the new chat should do first

Ask the owner, in one short message:
1. Which accounts exist (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook page, Pinterest, YouTube), their handles, follower counts, and what has been posted.
2. Time available per day, and whether they are comfortable showing their face or voice.
3. Whether they hold a physical sample of the product, and whether the oil bottle matches the page.
4. How much cash they need to free up, and by when (Black Friday is Nov 27; the sale ends Nov 10).
5. Whether they want to try creators (gifting, affiliate commission) or only their own posting.

Then propose a plan that fits the cash problem: content that is cheap to produce from the assets above, a posting schedule, how each post links to the store with tracking (UTMs, so orders show their source), and what to measure after a week. Keep it to a plan the owner can execute alone in under an hour a day.
