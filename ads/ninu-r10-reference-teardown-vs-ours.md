## 2. Why it may be working (my interpretation, not spec)

Hypotheses tied to the evidence above:

1. **The hook is the avatar's own question, shown and written.** A white text card from frame 0 and a face in distress
   by 1.5s [S01] means a muted viewer gets the whole hook. The topic is specific and embarrassing, so only the avatar
   stops.
2. **A second interrupt resolves the question absurdly.** At 4.12s a talking hair on a stage answers "HEY GIRL, IT'S
   ME" [S02]. Shame turns into entertainment, which lets her stay on a topic she'd normally skip.
3. **Blame is moved off her.** Five moves in a row: "YOU BLAME YOUR RAZOR/LUCK" → "I'M A SIGN" → "NOT PART WEREWOLF" →
   "IT'S JUST PCOS BABE" → "NOT BROKEN" [S08-S20]. That is the largest block of the ad.
4. **A new named cause.** She did not know "PCOS" or "androgens". The reveal gives the viewer something to take away.
5. **Symptom stacking widens the pool.** Five symptoms in two scenes [S15-S16], then "IT'S ALL ONE STORY" [S17]: anyone
   with any of them is pulled in and given one explanation.
6. **The product enters late and as a character** (54.3%, then named at 70.7%), after the cause is settled, so it
   reads as the answer, not the pitch.
7. **Cadence and pulse.** Nine cuts in the first 28s, a steady beat (about 132 BPM), loud mastering, one caption
   chunk every second or two. The ad never pauses to be sold to.
8. **Close:** hook callback [S31-S32], a long guarantee at 80% [S30], an end card with a real photo [S34].

## 6. Mechanism, and how it meets your avatar's pain

**Avatar evidence (two Reddit threads, verbatim):**
- Pain: "Nobody warned me about pulling out handfuls of hair in the shower"; "the temples/front of my hairline are the
  worst part… photos are brutal lately"; "To say it's humiliating is an understatement"; "Right here with you....in
  the mirror crying."
- Desire: "I just want to feel like myself again, honestly."
- Failed solutions: "changing shampoos, vitamins, satin pillowcases"; "Massage in castor oil, put on a shower cap";
  Nutrafol "$88/mo".
- Skepticism: "Did anything ACTUALLY help? (not influencer BS real mom experience)".
- Count: one commenter mentions oiling; none mention where the oil lands.

**What that says about the mechanism (interpretation):**
1. The reference's cause reveal fixes a thing she did not know. Ours is the same kind of reveal: oil put on hair does
   not reach the scalp. Nobody in the threads names this, so it is new to her. It is our "PCOS babe" moment.
2. The master doc's Avatar Deepening says that when the reader believes the wrong cause, or is in a vacuum, the ad
   should open by validating that she did everything right, and warns that "Opening with a correction when the reader
   is in a vacuum reads as an insult". That is the same permission device the reference uses (devices 29-30), and the one
   our R2 desire extraction already named: "I was never the problem. The method was."
3. Our video states the solution mechanism ("directly on the skin", plus the cross-section shot at 36-40s) but never the
   problem mechanism. The chore scene, the mess (oil down the neck, into the towel) and "hair doesn't absorb oil" were
   all cut for runtime. The mechanism therefore reads as a feature, not a reveal.
4. The pains she names (hair in the shower, hairline in photos, humiliation, crying in the mirror) do not appear
   anywhere in our video. The habit angle is a real converter (Ad C) but it is a smaller pain than the ones in her
   own words.

## 7. What this means for the next version

1. **Rebuild the first 4 seconds** to pass the 8-point test: a text card in frame 0 in her own words, a face in
   tension, a cut by 4-5s. Candidates, in order of fit with her language (all questions, none promise a result):
   - "Why is my castor oil not doing anything?" (solution-aware, matches her failed-solutions list)
   - "Why am I pulling out handfuls of hair in the shower?" (her verbatim pain, but check it against compliance first)
   - Personified shame object: the neglected oil bottle at the back of the shelf says "Hey girl. It's me. The one you
     swore you'd use every night." (the reference's "HEY GIRL, IT'S ME" move, using our Sunday and shelf story)
2. **Add the problem mechanism and the permission block** between the hook and the product: show oil landing on
   the hair, the neck and the towel, then name the reveal and absolve her ("It never reached your scalp. It wasn't you.").
   Aim for 25-30% of runtime, like the reference's 28.7%.
3. **Put the avatar in the words** (a postpartum or baby cue within 5s) and shift to second person. Ours is 1.1 per
   100 words against 9.5.
4. **Cut cadence in the first 30s** from 4 changes to about 8-9, with the product held back until the cause is set.
5. **Keep what worked:** the product fidelity, the Sunday callback, and the cool-to-warm grade shift as the payoff.
   Do not copy the reference's hormone, PCOS or "but not for long" claims.
6. **Pace:** a faster, steadier pulse would also fit the cadence better. This conflicts with "sung slowly", so decide
   whether that constraint is worth keeping.
