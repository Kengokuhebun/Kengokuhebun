# Round 1 long-form ad copy

These are the full-length primary text versions of the three test ads. Each runs
300-500 words — long enough to qualify the specific avatar, build belief in the
mechanism, and close. The short-form versions in `ninu-r1-test-ads.md` were
built as a trim of this copy and can remain for the ABO test; these long-form
versions are written for a second test pass, a landing-page advertorial, or any
placement where longer copy outperforms.

---

## Framework applied

**Breakthrough Advertising (Schwartz):** The postpartum hair market is saturated
with ingredient claims — biotin, collagen, rosemary, keratin. Every promise of
"grow your hair back" has been made. This is a sophisticated audience. She
doesn't need another benefit promise; she needs a new mechanism — the
delivery variable she hasn't considered yet. All three ads channel the existing
mass desire ("I want to feel like myself again, hair and all") and attach it to
the delivery tool as the hidden answer.

**Great Leads (Masterson/Forde):** Our avatar is Problem Aware → moving toward
Solution Aware. She knows she's shedding. She's been oiling. She doesn't know
the tool is the missing piece. This calls for a **Problem-Solution lead** (Ad A,
Ad B) or a **Story lead** (Ad C, mirroring Kitsch Vid 2's proven "because of
this little gadget" frame). The Rule of One: each ad carries one insight only —
the oil isn't getting to the scalp.

**Aarreign's Master Doc — Native Structure:**
- **Section 1:** Hook (1-3 line thumb-stopper) → Lead: relatable problem,
  "worst part isn't X, it's Y" reframe, rock-bottom moment.
- **Section 2:** Product Discovery + New Info on the Problem + New Info on the
  Solution + Results/proof.
- **Section 3:** Offer + Guarantee + CTA (the clear, unmistakable next step).

## Notes on framework application

### Why Problem-Solution for A and B (Great Leads)

The avatar is Problem Aware — she knows about postpartum shedding, she's already
oiling. She doesn't yet know the delivery tool is the missing variable. That
places her between Problem Aware and Solution Aware on Schwartz's five-level
scale. A direct Offer or Promise lead would skip past her current emotional state
("why would I buy a dispenser if I don't know delivery is the problem?"). The
Problem-Solution lead earns the pivot: first validate the problem she's living
("oil isn't working"), then name the hidden cause ("it's not getting to the
scalp"), then introduce the mechanism as the revelation.

### Why Story for C (Great Leads)

Ad C mirrors Kitsch Vid 2's winning "because of this little gadget, I actually
use my serums" frame — which is a Story lead. The promise is embedded in the
opening line: "I actually do it every night now." That's the outcome statement;
the story then earns it. Masterson calls this the Story lead's secret: readers
who resist a direct pitch will follow a story anywhere. The habit-failure angle
also unlocks a different pain from A and B — not "my hair is falling out," but
"I can't keep the routine I already believe in." Different entry point, same
product revelation.

### Why the market sophistication matters (Breakthrough Advertising)

The postpartum hair loss market has heard every ingredient claim. Biotin, collagen,
rosemary, minoxidil, rice water, keratin — if it's been claimed, she's seen the
ad. Schwartz's principle: when the market is this sophisticated, the new
mechanism is the only thing that cuts through. We don't lead with "grow your
hair back." We lead with a mechanism she hasn't considered: the delivery
variable. Every ad pivots on the same hidden insight — the oil isn't reaching
the scalp — because that's the new information the sophisticated market hasn't
been sold yet.

### Compliance notes (carry forward from ninu-r1-test-ads.md)

- No regrowth or hair-growth claims. These ads make no promise about hair
  growing; they promise a better delivery routine.
- No testimonial presentation unless the first-person narrative is a real
  person's story. Present as brand voice ("moms tell us…") if running as a
  brand ad with a fake persona.
- No personal-attributes language ("your bald spot," "your thinning hair").
  These ads name the problem in first person from the narrator's perspective,
  which is the safer frame.
