# R22: "One question" discovery story, rebuilt for Ninu

Written 2026-11-05. Reference: a very long (about 5,000 words) first-person advertorial for a diabetes nerve supplement. I used its **structure only**. Nothing from its claims, characters or product carries over (section 3). Ninu's angle, desire, avatar and the buyer language from `ninu-core-copy-resource-breakthrough-advertising.md` go in.

## 1. The reference's engine, beat by beat

| # | Beat in the reference | What it does | Ninu equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: a dramatic claim plus "ONE question" | Curiosity gap, promises a reveal | "I restarted my routine four times. Then I asked myself one question." |
| 2 | Specific scene with place and time | Pulls the reader in | A Sunday at 10pm, edge of the tub, towel on shoulders |
| 3 | The hidden cause shown by a physical moment (the foot she couldn't feel) | The reader recognises her own symptom | The towel is soaked, the part is barely damp |
| 4 | The "one question" asked | The turn | "Where is the oil actually going?" |
| 5 | Authority explains the mechanism with 3 analogies | Belief, "new information on the problem" | **No invented authority.** She answers it herself, with a list and one analogy |
| 6 | Alternatives mocked by analogy | Concentration | Dropper, fingers, reminders, another bottle |
| 7 | A precise spec checklist ("the whole specification") | Makes the product the only one that qualifies | Her five needs, and the dispenser meeting each |
| 8 | Honest "week one is nothing much" timeline | Believability | Night 1, week 1, week 3 (one miss), week 5 |
| 9 | "What it won't do" and who it's for | Deadly Sincerity, filters buyers | Included |
| 10 | Offer with guarantee, then urgency | Close | Offer block; urgency only if real |

## 2. Hooks (the image or first line)

1. i restarted my scalp oil routine 4 times. then i asked myself one question.
2. the one question that fixed my scalp oil routine
3. where is your scalp oil actually going?
4. i checked where my scalp oil was going. not on my scalp.
5. sunday was the start. 4 sundays in a row.

Hooks 1 and 3 are closest to the reference. All five name "scalp oil" in the first line, so they filter for buyers. Test one at a time, as before.

## 3. What I deliberately did not carry over

- The invented doctor, the invented colleague and the "I called the company myself" verification story. Ninu's copy has no fabricated authority.
- Any disease, medical or treatment claim, and any bashing of medications or doctors. The reference tells readers its product does what drugs don't, and that she halved her medication. That is dangerous, and not what Ninu is about.
- The court case and the statistics. They can't be checked.
- Before and after numbers, test scores and "doctor was amazed" scenes. Ninu has no results to report, and its guardrail is behaviour and delivery only.
- The "backordered, small batches, if it's in stock don't wait" line. That is invented scarcity.
- "Made in the USA", "third-party tested" and the specification claims. Unverified for Ninu.

## 4. The rebuild (primary text)

Fixed with the other ads: headline "Your messy oil routine's worst nightmare", description "Free US shipping · 30-day guarantee", CTA Shop Now with the tagged link. Gift lines stay out until the gifts are live.

```
I restarted my scalp oil routine four times in six months.

The fifth time, I asked myself one question I had never asked. It's the reason I haven't skipped a night since.

I'm 34. My daughter was five months old.

It was a Sunday, 10pm. I was sitting on the edge of the tub with a towel around my shoulders, a bottle of castor oil in one hand and a hand towel in my lap for the drips. Down the hall the baby monitor crackled.

I'd told myself Sunday would be the start. It had been the start four other Sundays.

I had the castor oil. I had the rosemary. I believed in the routine. Every time, I'd do it four nights in a row. Then the baby would be up three times, I'd get into bed with wet hands, and I'd skip it. Then I'd skip a second night. The bottle would drift to the back of the shelf and I'd tell myself I'd start over Sunday.

Every. Single. Sunday.

That night I finished a section and looked down. The towel was soaked. My neck was shiny. I put my fingers on my part to check, and it was barely damp.

That's when I asked it.

Where is the oil actually going?

On my fingers. Down my neck. Into the towel. Onto my pillowcase on the nights I didn't wash it out. Everywhere except the one place I wanted it.

Nobody had ever asked me that. Everyone said do it every night. Nobody asked how I was applying it.

I'd spent six months trying to be more disciplined about a method that left the oil on everything but my scalp. It was like watering the leaves when you meant the roots.

And I'd tried the other fixes. A reminder on my phone, which just made me feel guilty at 10pm. Another bottle, which went to the back of the shelf with the first one. A reminder gets you to start. An easier routine gets you to keep going.

So I made a list on my phone. Not features. What I actually needed.

The oil has to land on my scalp, along the part, not on my hair.

I have to be able to do it with one hand. The other one usually has a baby on it.

I need to see how much I'm using, so I stop pouring half the bottle.

It has to work with the oil I already own. I wasn't buying another bottle of anything.

It has to take about two minutes. If it took ten, it was Sunday again.

I found a dispenser with hollow silicone tips. It's called Ninu. It checked every line.

Here's how it works. You part your hair. You press the tips against your scalp along the part. The oil releases right there, on the skin. Nothing runs. Nothing drips. You move down the part and do the next one. Clear chamber, 1 to 4ml markings. One hand. About two minutes.

The first night I thought: oh. This is what it's supposed to feel like. I looked at my shoulders. I hadn't used a towel.

I want to be honest about what came next, because nothing dramatic did.

Week one. It's oil on a scalp. No rush, no tingle. The difference was that I did it. By the end of the week I hadn't skipped a night. That had never happened.

Week two. The bottle was on the nightstand, not the back of the shelf. I stopped dreading 10pm.

Week three. The baby was up four times and I fell asleep with my shoes on. I missed a night. I did it the next night. That was the whole plan. There was nothing to restart.

Week five. It was automatic, same as washing my face. Not because I'd become more disciplined. Because the method was wrong before and this one isn't.

Here's what it won't do. It won't make your oil work harder and it won't do the routine for you. It makes the routine easy enough that you actually do it. If you've never oiled your scalp, or you don't want to, this isn't for you.

If you already have a bottle and a good intention, here's the kit. The dispenser, 60ml of scalp oil (about a month of nights) and a satin sleep bonnet. $49.99, with free US shipping. That's about $1.67 a night to start.

It's $49.99 until Nov 10 for our early Black Friday sale. On Nov 11 it goes to $62.49.

And you can run the same check I did. Tonight, put the towel on out of habit, use the dispenser, and look at the towel. If the routine isn't easier and the oil isn't landing where I said it would, you have 30 days to get your money back.

I was never the problem. The method was.

The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit.

Make tonight the easy one. Tap Shop Now.
```

## 6. Length and where it fits

About 840 words (the reference is about six times longer). I cut length on purpose. The reference's urgency comes from a limb at risk; Ninu's is a routine, so a long escalation attracts readers who relate to the story, not buyers (resource section 3.2).

Where it fits in testing: this is concept #9 in the diversity matrix, a long-form "discovery" structure for the same angle. Run it after the R16 result, alongside the long advertorial (R17-C), as the format test. Not before.
