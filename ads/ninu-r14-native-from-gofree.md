**Shared by both (the house format, as measured):**
1. A first-person mother narrator and a named situation in the first sentence.
2. A villain that is the common set-up (flowers; backpacks, diaper bags, totes), named with examples.
3. A list of small things early, with some entries paid off later.
4. The product named as a category first; the brand late or not in the prose.
5. A third party's doubt or reaction quoted ("how did you know about the hair ties.", "that's it?").
6. A "stays put" claim ("installed things don't wander", "nothing in this bag moves").
7. A one-hand or hands-free reach.
8. Time-stamped proof (3:41 text; five clock-timed uses).
9. No price, guarantee or urgency in the story, and a link at the very end.

Where GoFree differs: shorter (645 versus 1,103), no mechanism explanation (the narrator declines: "I don't know. I genuinely don't know."), a refrain that carries the ad ("That's it." ×5), five demonstrations that together are 35.8% of the words.

- **The photo shows what the copy claims** (a child's hand in hers, [¶12] "I held my daughter's hand through every line", and the bag worn on the body) without any caption.

## 3. The process to reproduce it as a static native

1. Dissect the copy and the image (done).
2. Choose what the image has to prove: here, "the product is in use in a real moment and her hands are free".
3. Translate the scene to your avatar and your product: not a theme park but the one place the routine lives, the bedside at night.
4. Shoot it as a real phone photo of your real product (best), or generate an illustrative one from your master product image.
5. No text overlay, no logo, no caption in the image (the original has none); the primary text does the selling.
6. Export at 4:5 (1080 × 1350) and 9:16 (1080 × 1920), safe zone for the 9:16 (keep the product between 14% and 80% of the height).

## 5. The long-form primary text (450 words)

In the GoFree beat order. 16 of 63 sentences are verbatim Ad C (23% of the words); the other 47 are connective lines from Ad C's and Ad A's facts.
No em dashes, no question marks, no exclamation marks.

```
Okay I need to talk about scalp oil.

Not the biotin. Not the collagen. Not the gummies. The routine situation.

Because if you've tried to do it every night with a baby in the house, you know the routine is what makes you quit.

I've sat on the edge of the tub for thirty minutes. I've wrapped a towel around my shoulders AND worried about the pillowcase AND wiped down the sink. I've pushed the bottle to the back of the shelf and told myself Sunday.

Month five postpartum. One baby. Up three times a night. I set the routine up like I was going to war. Because I was.

The castor oil. The rosemary. A towel for my shoulders. The cleanup after. Thirty minutes I did not have. Wet hands I would carry to bed, because if you believe in the routine you will carry the wet hands to bed, that is the law.

Then I found a dispenser with hollow silicone tips.

It's about the size of my palm. I put it on the nightstand.

That's it.

Here's what happened over the first month:

Night one. I pressed the tips against my scalp along the part and the oil released right there, directly on the skin. One hand. Two minutes. I thought: oh. This is what it's supposed to feel like.

No towel. No wet hands. No cleanup.

End of week one. I hadn't skipped a night. That had never happened.

A few weeks in, the routine was automatic, same as washing my face. The dispenser was still on the nightstand where I put it on night one. Nothing about this routine needs to move.

Every Sunday I used to tell myself I'd start over. I never started over Sunday. This time I didn't have to.

I'm not going to pretend it's complicated. Hair doesn't absorb oil. Skin does. Nights of working it down the strands, and almost none of it reached my scalp. The tool I'd been using was wrong and this one isn't.

I use it the same way every night. I press the tips against my scalp, I wait two minutes, I go to sleep. And every night I think: that's it. That's the whole routine.

The Ninu Kit comes with the dispenser, 60ml scalp oil, and a satin bonnet for $49.99. Free US shipping and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Early Black Friday price: $49.99 until Nov 10. It goes to $62.49 on Nov 11.

I was never the problem. The method was.

The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit.

That's it.

Make tonight the easy one. Try the Ninu Kit risk-free for 30 days.
```

**Headline / description / CTA** (keep the control's so only the creative changes): "Your messy oil routine's worst nightmare" / "Free US shipping · 30-day guarantee" / Shop Now.

## 7. The native photo: brief and prompt

**What it has to show:** the real product in use in a real moment, at the bedside, with no text.

**Preferred: shoot it (a real phone photo).** Your own sample, your own nightstand.
- Phone camera, no filters, no edit beyond a crop. Evening lamp light, slightly imperfect.
- Frame: from the edge of the bed at sitting height, looking at the nightstand. The dispenser upright, centred, about a quarter of the frame height (the pink bag in the original is that size).
- Also in the frame (all real, nothing bought for the shot): a baby monitor with its light on, the phone face-up showing a time near 10:04 PM, a glass of water, a hair tie, the satin bonnet folded.
- No hands needed, no face, no baby, no text, no logo overlay, no caption. Take 6 shots, pick one.
- Export 4:5 (1080 × 1350) and 9:16 (1080 × 1920).

**Fallback: an illustrative generated image** (attach your white-background product master image so the product is correct):
