## L1: Belly Band / Proof-Stack (control)

Word count: 781

### Primary Text

My 5-year-old asks for this by name. Plug it in, hand him the markers — that's the whole setup.

I am writing this at 10 pm with both boys finally down, so I will keep it plain.

On a Wednesday in August, at 5:51 pm, I added up what I had spent since January trying to keep Noah and Eli off the iPad before dinner. Noah was crying for Bluey. Eli was pulling at the oven handle. I made the list on my phone with one thumb, standing at the stove.

Here it is.

Kinetic sand kit, $38. Fifteen minutes on day one. Eight minutes on day two.

Magnet tiles, $89. He built one tower. It fell. It never came back out of the box.

Melissa & Doug easel, $142. Used three times. It lives in the garage.

Target water table, $118. Outdoors only. Good from October to March, and it was August.

Pop-it fidget, free. One afternoon.

Educational tablet, $179. That is a tablet.

Cup-and-ball from Grandpa, $12. I hid it.

Sticker chart from Etsy, $6. Noah peeled off the stickers. Then he ate one.

A tent, $65. One week.

Those nine came to $649. The rest was smaller things I stopped writing down. The total was $847 since January.

Every one of those was a Saturday morning of thinking this one would do it. I read the box and watched the video. Some I set up before he woke so it would be ready when he came out. None of it held.

I looked at the number for a while. Eli was still on the oven handle. I turned the burner off and gave Noah the iPad.

Two days later, a mum from preschool pickup texted me a photo. Her son was at her kitchen bench, drawing a dinosaur on something that glowed. Under the photo she wrote: "I know. I did not believe it either. It is the first toy my kid asks for by name."

I did not believe her either. I bought it anyway, because I was out of other ideas. That was September.

Here is what it is. A clear acrylic panel on a white stand with a light inside. Eight fluorescent markers. One USB cable. Noah draws on the panel and the lines glow. When he is done, I wipe it with a damp cloth and he draws again.

The first night, I stood at the stove and watched him instead of cooking. He drew a dinosaur with a very long tail. The tail glowed green. He looked up at me like he had done it on purpose, which he had.

It is not a screen. There is no app, no sound and no autoplay. If he stops drawing, it does nothing. It just sits there. That is the part I had wrong for eight months. The iPad wins because it does the work for him. This works the other way. He makes the glow, so he holds his own attention. Nothing holds it for him. Not a server farm in Seattle.

If your list looks like mine, it was not you. Most toys need a kid to bring the interest. A screen brings its own.

I know what you are thinking, because I thought it. He will play with it for a week and then it will go in the garage with the easel. I bought it in September. He has used it every day since. Not once because I asked.

Last Tuesday, 5:41 pm. Spaghetti and garlic bread. I set the board on the kitchen bench, plugged in the USB cable and put out four markers. Noah climbed up on the stool. I cooked for 22 minutes. No iPad. No negotiating. He drew a rocket, then a dog, then wiped it and started over. Eli sat on my hip for part of it and drew one orange line. He was very pleased with it. When I called him to dinner, he brought the board to the table and drew his meal before he ate it.

It is called Glowling, and it is sold only from their own site. Amazon has listings, but they are not from Glowling, so the deal and the warranty do not apply there. From Black Friday through Cyber Monday it is Buy 2, Get 1 Free. That is $69.98 for three, with a free tracing book. If it does not work in your house, you have 30 days to send it back to the brand for your money.

That night I wiped the old ledger off my phone and started a new one. It has one line.

The glow board.

Noah asked for it by name again tonight.
