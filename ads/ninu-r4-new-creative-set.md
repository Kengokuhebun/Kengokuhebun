# R4 — New creative ad set (4 ads: 1 swipe + 3 natives)

Same proven DNA as the Ad C control (which has now converted twice), applied across four
new visual concepts: one reproduced from a competitor's winning design, three original
native ideas. This is not a hook-isolation test like R2 — each ad is a genuinely different
creative, body copy included, built from the same angle, desire, emotional driver and
mechanism.

---

## 1. The DNA being carried across all four

| | |
|---|---|
| **Angle** | The tool fixes the habit problem, not the hair problem. She already believes in oiling. The mess/friction is what kills the routine. A precise, contained tool removes the reason to quit. |
| **Desire** | To be the kind of mom who actually keeps up with her own routines postpartum — identity relief, not a hair-outcome claim. |
| **Emotional driver** | Quiet domestic shame and small relational friction (what the mess costs her — with her husband, with the baby's space, in front of the world) that eventually kills the habit. |
| **Rule of One (kept verbatim in all four closes — this line has now converted twice)** | *"The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit."* |
| **Mechanism (identical across all four)** | Hollow silicone tips press to the scalp along the part; oil releases on the skin, not on the hair. Clear chamber, 1–4ml markings. One hand, two minutes, works with the oil she already has. |
| **Offer (identical across all four)** | The Ninu Kit: dispenser + 60ml scalp oil + satin bonnet, $49.99. Free US shipping, 30-day money-back guarantee. |

**What's new per ad:** the hook, the lead, and what specifically counts as "the reason to
quit" in each story. Each of the three natives turns out to be a literal visualization of
that phrase — a stained pillow, a confused husband, a hidden scarf. The swipe ad is the
odd one out by design: it's a demonstration shot, not a consequence shot, so its hook
leans declarative rather than confessional.

**Frameworks applied (BTA / Great Leads / Master Doc, same as prior rounds):**
- Market stays at Awareness Level 3 / Sophistication Level 3 — no ingredient claims, the
  mechanism (delivery, not formula) is still the news.
- Lead types: Ad 1 = Proclamation/demonstration (the image does most of the work). Ads 2–4
  = Story leads, each opening on a specific dateable scene, per Great Leads' rule that a
  story lead earns the reveal rather than stating it.
- Native structure per ad: Hook → Lead → Failed Solutions → Discovery → Mechanism →
  Product Timeline → Rule of One → Two-Roads CTA → Offer.
- AARREIGN chiefing checklist applied to all four: no em-dashes, contractions throughout,
  failed-solutions section present, product timeline present, two-roads close, no hair
  regrowth or medical claims — only behavior, consistency and mess claims.
- **Grade 5 reading level pass:** short sentences, plain everyday words. "Migrating" became
  "the stain wasn't even on my side anymore." "Applying it" became "putting it on." No word
  in any of the four that a 10-year-old wouldn't already know.
- **Emotional bookend pass:** the hook's exact image now returns in the closing CTA, not a
  generic consequence. Ad 1 opens and closes on "right here, not your towel, not your
  pillow." Ad 2 opens and closes on waking up / checking the pillow. Ad 3 opens and closes
  on the husband's hand being clean or not. Ad 4 opens and closes on wrapping (or not
  wrapping) the scarf. The reader starts and ends in the same feeling, not a different one.

## 3. Pre-launch checks

- **Image 1 (swipe):** confirm the generated drip shot doesn't read as too close to
  Kitsch's original composition — different background texture/crop, our own ml-marking
  typography, our own cap shape if it differs from theirs, enough to be clearly your own
  product photography, not a visual copy.
- **Images 2–4 (natives):** run each through the Phase 5 test before finalizing — "would
  this make sense posted with 'ugh, dealing with this again' as the caption?" If a
  generation comes back looking composed or pretty, push the prompt harder rather than
  accept it.
- **Ad 3 (the crib):** double check the generated image doesn't show the baby at all —
  prompt is written to keep it to hand + hair + crib rail only, but verify on generation.
  Showing an infant in an "oily hair near the crib" context is the one concept here with
  real downside if it reads as a safety/cleanliness concern rather than a relatable
  domestic-mess beat. If it reads at all alarming rather than funny-relatable in testing,
  cut this ad before the other three.
- **No regrowth/medical claims** — confirmed clean across all four; only behavior,
  consistency, and mess claims are made.
- First-person framing on all four — same honesty note as every prior round: these need
  to run as real UGC or clearly as brand voice, not presented as verified customer
  testimonials unless they are.
