## 2. The process to reproduce it as a static native

1. **Dissect the ad** (done): paragraph index, beat map, loops, devices, claims, fixed versus swappable.
2. **Pick the static carrier.** The static is not the ad. It is one moment from the copy that works alone. The master doc's native image rules (Native Images master prompt) require that the image look like something a person posted, that the product stay out of the image, and that nothing in it be fabricated. The beat that works alone here is the list of small things [¶6].
3. **Pick the native format.** The skill's iOS Notes format fits "a private, unpolished, documenting moment". It also suits a list.
4. **Write the on-screen text in the format's own voice** (lowercase, typed in a rush), using only lines that already appear in your proven copy.
5. **Build one HTML per ratio** with chrome at the top and bottom and the real content in the middle (1:1, 9:16, 1.91:1), render at exact pixels, and read every render back.
6. **Pair it with the copy.** The image carries the hook; the primary text does the selling.
7. **Test one variable at a time** (section 7).

```
Baby up three times.
Wet hands, in bed.
Skipped my scalp oil again.

I had the castor oil. I had the rosemary. I believed in the routine. I just couldn't make myself do it consistently.

I want to be careful here, because I never stopped believing in the oil. The oil was fine. What I couldn't keep up was everything around it.

Somebody has to wash her hands. Somebody has to wrap a towel around her shoulders to protect her shirt, change the pillowcase, wipe the sink. That somebody is me, at 10pm.

A chore at 10pm after a full day with a baby is a chore that gets skipped.

I'd do it four nights in a row and then the baby would be up three times and I'd get into bed with wet hands and just not do it. Then I'd skip a second night. The bottle would migrate to the back of the shelf and I'd tell myself I'd start over Sunday. I never started over Sunday.

Somewhere in the middle of all those Sundays, I stopped asking how to be more disciplined. I started asking what the routine would have to look like to need nothing from me.

No towel. No wet hands. No cleanup.

And here's the other thing nobody mentions. Everyone asked how the baby was sleeping. Nobody asked what I needed.

I just wanted to feel like myself again. The scalp oil was the one thing I was doing for me, and it was the one thing I kept skipping.

So I made a list on my phone of what actually stopped me. It wasn't the oil.

The bottle at the back of the shelf. Wet hands and a clean pillowcase. The towel. The cleanup. 10pm.

Small things. Bedside things. The things nobody tells you about when they tell you to do it every night.

What I decided was that I didn't need more discipline. I needed less work. I wasn't going to get better at a bad method. I was going to find a better one and leave it where I actually end up, next to the bed.

Not another thing to manage. Installed.

Then I found a dispenser with hollow silicone tips.

You press the tips against the scalp along the part and the oil releases right there, directly on the skin. Nothing runs. Nothing drips. Clear chamber, 1 to 4ml markings. One hand. Two minutes. Works with the oil I already had.

Not one extra step. That was on purpose. I had enough steps.

It's called Ninu. I set it on the nightstand where my hand already goes, and went to bed.

The first night I used it I thought: oh. This is what it's supposed to feel like.

Turns out I'd been putting it in the wrong place. Hair doesn't absorb oil. Skin does. Nights of working it down the strands, and almost none of it reached my scalp.

And a dropper needs both hands. A towel needs a plan. A routine that needs both gets skipped.

By the end of the first week I hadn't skipped a night. That had never happened. A few weeks in the routine was automatic, same as washing my face. Not because I'd become more disciplined. Because the tool I'd been using was wrong and this one isn't.

I was never the problem. The method was.

The habit was always there. The routine just needed to be easier than the reason to quit.

Make tonight the easy one. Try the Ninu Kit risk-free for 30 days.
```

## 6. Pre-launch gates

- **First-person voice.** The story reads as one mom's account. Run it as brand voice or UGC, not as a verified testimonial. Same standing note as every earlier round.
- **Price lines must be true.** "$49.99 until Nov 10, then $62.49" requires the kit to actually be $62.49 from Nov 11 (Shopify price and the page's `TIER` kit price).
- **Ebook left out** of the copy and the image, since delivery is not built.
- **Claims:** behaviour and delivery claims only ("I hadn't skipped a night", "the oil releases right there"). No regrowth, shedding or medical claim. "almost none of it reached my scalp" is already in the running Ad C.
- **Link:** the copy has no URL. Meta's CTA button carries the tagged link, so orders keep their ad ID. A typed link would lose the attribution.
- **Funnel gate** from `ninu-r12-store-diagnosis-woa.md`: PayPal pending orders, and tracked Ad ID to order checks.
