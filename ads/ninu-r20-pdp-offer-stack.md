# R20: The PDP offer stack (gifts shown before add-to-cart)

Written 2026-11-04. Responds to your brainstorm. Builds on `ninu-r19-offer-evolution.md` (costs and supplier questions) and replaces its "free batana oil" idea as the lead gift, because gifts that keep the routine going fit the winning angle better than another oil.

## 1. Where I agree, and what the master doc says

You're right that a gift in the cart is a gift nobody weighs up before deciding. It has to be on the page, above the button, and in the sticky bar. Add-to-cart is the leak, and the cart is after the leak.

What the master doc requires of an offer on the page:
- Gifts must be real. PDP image slide 1: "Without confirmed bonuses, show the product alone or its verified contents." Slide 9: do not fabricate a complementary product. So every gift needs a real sample and a real photo, not an AI-drawn item.
- Offer block: pulled exactly from the product page, "free gift or free shipping callouts if real," per-use cost math if it holds ($1.67 a night to start), guarantee, "no manufactured urgency unless the brand truth gives you a real deadline."
- CTA rule: button text names the offer, one-line guarantee underneath, sticky bar on mobile.
- Never invent prices, and don't turn supplier pricing into a retail price.
- Its own diagnosis rule: "Good clicks but weak conversion means the belief shift did not complete... Add-to-cart but no purchase means risk, offer, urgency." So the doc reads weak add-to-cart as a belief problem first. A visible gift stack is a fair bet, but it doesn't replace the copy and proof work.

## 2. Your list, scored

The test I'd use (from Schwartz and your angle): does the gift serve the one promise, a routine easy enough to keep? Gifts that add steps or make claims work against it.

| Idea | Fit with the promise | Perceived value, cost | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Silicone scalp massager | Medium. Fits "scalp ritual", but it is an extra step | Good. Light and cheap to ship. Common product, so some buyers own one | "Stimulates blood flow" is a physiology claim. Don't use it | **Yes**, framed as a wash-day tool |
| Derma roller | Contradicts "one hand, two minutes" | High, but for the wrong reason | Hair-growth framing. Microneedling at home breaks skin; using oil after it raises irritation and infection risk. Postpartum buyers. Ad and payment policy exposure (not verified) | **No** |
| Bamboo comb | Low. Another step | Low, and it adds weight | Low | **Skip** |
| Waterproof pouch / wash bag | **High.** "I set it on the nightstand where my hand already goes" is in your own copy. A pouch makes the kit a physical, bedside thing | Good, and it makes the offer look like a kit | Needs a real item and photo | **Yes** (call it a bedside pouch) |
| Microfiber hair wrap | Medium. Towel pain is a real theme, though a hair wrap doesn't stop oil on shoulders | Good, but bulky to ship | Low | **Maybe later** |
| 30-Day Scalp Transformation Protocol | Concept fits, the name doesn't | Zero cost | "Transformation", growth tracking and diet tips are claims and drift into medical territory | **Yes, renamed:** a 30-night routine tracker |

On the tracker: you already wrote "The Scalp Routine That Sticks" (`pdp/ninu-ebook-scalp-routine-that-sticks.md`). A tracker that ticks off nights done, not hair outcomes, is the habit angle in printable form. Its delivery isn't built yet, so it can't be advertised until it is.

## 3. The stack I'd put on the page

**Ninu Kit, $49.99, with three free gifts:**
1. Silicone scalp massager
2. Bedside pouch
3. 30-night routine tracker (digital)

Plus what is already in the kit: dispenser, 60ml oil, satin sleep bonnet.

Cost cap: total gift cost (including extra shipping) of about $5 or less. Past that, the table in R19 says conversion has to rise 21% or more just to hold profit per click. Rough guesses for the physical items are about $1 to $3 each, but those are not supplier numbers.

If a supplier quote shows the gifts cost more than the cap, drop the pouch first, then the massager. The tracker costs nothing and stays.

## 5. How to build it (two options)

| Option | How | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Gifts included in the kit | The page lists them; the supplier packs them; you add a line in the fulfilment note | No cart logic, no discount rules, nothing to break | Needs the supplier or your tool to know the new contents |
| B. $0 line items | Each gift is a product at $0 via an automatic discount, added by the page code | Clear on the order | More moving parts in your custom code |

Start with A. Tell me how orders reach the supplier (an app, manual orders, spreadsheet) and I'll confirm.

## 6. Gates before anything goes live

1. Supplier quote and samples for the massager and the pouch: cost, same-parcel shipping, lead time, photos.
2. Real photos of the gifts. No AI-invented items.
3. The tracker built (Canva) and its delivery set (link in the order confirmation email, or a digital-download app).
4. Price honesty: the strike price and the sale date logic. The gifts should not be funded by raising the kit price. If the price goes to $62.49 on Nov 11, decide whether the gifts stay.
5. Fix the oil bottle image and ingredient question from R19 first, because the box photo shows it.

## 7. Sequencing

The gifts need sourcing (days), so run the R16 copy test now. Launch the gift stack as the next window, with the PDP block as the one change. Running both together would blur which one moved add-to-carts.

Expect modest results: the stack needs a 13% to 21% rise in conversion to pay for itself.
